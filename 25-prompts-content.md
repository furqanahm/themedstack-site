## How to use this pack

Every prompt below follows three rules:

1. **Assign a role.** "Act as my surgical reg." — better answers than no role.
2. **Give it constraints.** Year level, time pressure, marking rubric, word limit.
3. **Force pushback.** Tell the AI to challenge you, not validate you.

When prompts have `[brackets]`, replace them with your actual situation. Don't just run them blank.

## I. OSCE & Clinical Skills (1–6)

### 01 · OSCE Patient Simulator
> Act as a [54-year-old male / 27-year-old female] presenting to [ED / GP / ward] with [chief complaint]. Stay in character as the patient throughout. Respond with realistic emotion, pain level, and detail — only give information when I ask the right question. After 8 minutes of simulated history, break character and mark me on:
> – Open vs closed questions
> – OPQRST coverage
> – Red flag screening
> – Empathy / ICE (ideas, concerns, expectations)
> – What I missed.

### 02 · OSCE Examiner Drill
> Act as an OSCE examiner for [year level] medical students. Give me a station: [chest pain / paeds rash / mental state exam / dehydration assessment]. Tell me the candidate instructions, the time limit, and start a stopwatch in your responses. After I run through the station in plain text, mark me strictly against the rubric and tell me what would have failed me.

### 03 · Differential Diagnosis Drill
> I'm presenting a case: [40F, sudden-onset right-sided chest pain, SOB, 2/10 calf swelling]. Don't give me the answer. Walk me through differentials in order of likelihood, force me to justify why each is more or less likely with each piece of info. Stop me if I anchor too early. End with the investigation that would distinguish your top 3.

### 04 · Cranial Nerve Exam Reset
> Walk me through a complete cranial nerve examination as if I've forgotten everything. Use a memorable mnemonic for the order. For each CN, give me: the test, what a normal response looks like, what a positive finding suggests (1–2 differentials), and the most common mistake students make. Keep it under 800 words.

### 05 · MSE (Mental State Exam) Frame
> Generate three psychiatric vignettes — one [psychotic / mood / personality] presentation each. For each, tell me what I should comment on under the standard MSE headings (appearance, behaviour, speech, mood, affect, thought form, thought content, perception, cognition, insight, judgement). Then give me a worked example MSE write-up I can copy the structure of.

### 06 · Handover Practice (ISBAR)
> Pretend I'm calling you, the on-call reg, about a deteriorating patient: [bed 4, post-op day 2 cholecystectomy, now febrile + tachycardic]. Force me to deliver an ISBAR handover. Interrupt me if I skip a section, get my SBAR out of order, or fail to make a request. Then mark my handover.

## II. SAQs & Written Exams (7–11)

### 07 · SAQ Generator (Year-Level Calibrated)
> Generate 6 short-answer questions on [topic: e.g. β-blockers / acid-base / diabetic ketoacidosis] at the level of a [Australian Year 3 MD / GAMSAT / final-year MD] student. Mix MOA, contraindications, side effects, monitoring, and clinical scenarios. Give marks per question, totalling 30. Don't show me the answers yet.

### 08 · SAQ Marker
> I'm going to write my answer to: "[paste SAQ]". Mark it strictly against an Australian medical school rubric for [year level]. For every mark I miss, tell me the exact phrase or concept I should have written. Don't be nice — fail me if I deserve it.

### 09 · Exam Crystalliser
> Take this dense topic — [topic, e.g. complement cascade] — and crystallise it into the absolute minimum I need to remember to answer any reasonable [BSc Med / MD] exam question on it. Output: 1 mnemonic, 5 facts, 1 high-yield diagram described in words.

### 10 · Past Paper Pattern Spotter
> Here are the last 5 years of [exam name]'s [topic] questions. [Paste them.] Find the patterns: which subtopics repeat, which never appear, what the examiner's favourite angle is. Predict the 3 most likely topics for the next exam.

### 11 · Active Recall Question Bank
> Convert these notes into a set of 30 active-recall questions: [paste notes]. Mix recall (what is X?), application (a patient with X presents with Y, what's next?), and synthesis (compare X and Y). Format as Anki-ready cloze cards.

## III. Pharm & Mechanism (12–14)

### 12 · Drug Class in 60 Seconds
> Teach me [drug class] like I'm a final-year MD student at 11pm the night before pharm. Cover: MOA in one sentence, indications, the 3 contraindications I'll get tested on, the most common side effect, the dangerous-but-rare side effect, monitoring, and one Australian-specific note (PBS, TGA, common script). Under 300 words.

### 13 · Drug Interaction Detective
> Patient is on [drug list]. They've been started on [new drug]. Walk me through every clinically meaningful interaction — pharmacokinetic vs pharmacodynamic — and tell me what I'd monitor, change, or stop. Cite the mechanism for each.

### 14 · "Why does this drug do this?" Drill
> Don't just tell me [drug] causes [side effect]. Walk me from the receptor / enzyme it acts on, through the physiology, to the clinical manifestation, in plain English. End with: "the reason this matters in exams is…"

## IV. Pathway, Career & Strategy (15–18)

### 15 · Med School Application Audit (UCAT/GAMSAT/Portfolio)
> I'm applying to [Australian undergrad MD / postgrad MD / IMG entry] in [year]. My current stats: [GPA, UCAT/GAMSAT, prereqs, ECs]. Audit me ruthlessly. What's competitive, what's a weakness, and what are the 3 highest-leverage things I can do in the next [3 / 6 / 12] months. Be specific, not generic.

### 16 · MMI / Medical Interview Drill
> Run me through [5 / 10] MMI-style stations covering ethics, communication, role-play, and personal insight. After each one I respond, mark me using the standard MMI rubric (knowledge, structure, empathy, professionalism). End with a debrief on the patterns in my weakest stations.

### 17 · Specialty Career Probe
> I'm a [year level] med student considering [specialty]. Without sugarcoating: what does training in Australia actually look like (length, exams, geography, lifestyle), what are the 3 things people don't tell you, and what kind of person actually thrives in it? Compare to [alternative specialty] honestly.

### 18 · Reflection Generator (for portfolios)
> I had a clinical encounter today: [paste 3-line summary]. Help me draft a Gibbs reflective cycle entry I can put in my portfolio — Description, Feelings, Evaluation, Analysis, Conclusion, Action Plan. Sound like a real student, not a corporate report. Keep it under 400 words.

## V. Study Systems & Productivity (19–22)

### 19 · Anki Card Forge
> Convert [paste notes / topic outline] into Anki cards. Use cloze deletions, atomic facts (one fact per card), and avoid yes/no cards. Each card should be answerable in under 8 seconds. Output as plain text I can paste straight into Anki, one card per line, cloze syntax.

### 20 · Rotation Cheat Sheet
> I'm starting [rotation: e.g. Gen Med, ED, Surg, O&G] tomorrow. Give me a one-page cheat sheet: most common presentations to expect, the 5 most-asked questions on ward rounds, the bare-minimum exam I should be slick at, and 3 things to ask my reg on day one to look engaged.

### 21 · Spaced Revision Schedule Builder
> I have [N] weeks until [exam name]. The syllabus covers [list topics]. Generate a week-by-week revision schedule using spaced repetition principles. Include: which topic to tackle each week, when to revisit (1 / 7 / 30 day rule), and where to slot active recall vs past papers vs sleep.

### 22 · Mind Map This Topic
> Create a hierarchical text-only mind map of [topic]. Use indentation to show levels. Start with the central concept, branch to mechanism / clinical / investigation / management, and stop at depth 4. I'll redraw it visually myself — your job is the structure.

## VI. Research, Writing & Late-Night Edge (23–25)

### 23 · Paper Demolisher
> I've got [paste abstract or upload PDF] for journal club. Critique it across: study design, population, intervention, comparator, outcomes, statistics, conflicts of interest, and external validity to Australian practice. Tell me the 3 questions I should ask in the meeting to look sharp without sounding arrogant.

### 24 · Systematic Review Helper
> I'm running a systematic review on: "[research question]". Suggest a PICO breakdown, a search strategy across PubMed + Embed + Cochrane (with MeSH terms and Boolean operators), inclusion/exclusion criteria, and the PRISMA sections I'll need to fill out. Don't write the review for me — scaffold it.

### 25 · Pre-Exam Mind State
> It's the night before [exam]. I'm spiralling. Don't motivate me with platitudes. Tell me: what to study in the next 4 hours (high-yield only), what to STOP studying, when to sleep, what to eat in the morning, and one cognitive reframe that'll actually work for exam-day anxiety. Keep it under 200 words.
