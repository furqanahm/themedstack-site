# Research Operating Manual

## The Full Research Pathway

Most students do research backwards. They ask for "a project" before they know what kind of project they can actually complete.

ResearchStack uses this pathway:

1. Choose your research lane
2. Select realistic project types
3. Build a supervisor/contact list
4. Send tailored outreach
5. Clarify the project in a meeting
6. Confirm governance, role, timeline, and output
7. Build the project assets
8. Create tables and figures early
9. Write the abstract/manuscript
10. Format and submit
11. Track CV evidence

## Stage 1: Choose Your Research Lane

Pick 1-2 lanes only.

Good examples:

- general surgery audits
- emergency department digital health
- medical education in clinical students
- psychiatry case reports
- rural health service evaluation
- paediatric quality improvement

Bad examples:

- surgery
- AI
- cardiology
- research
- anything publishable

Your lane should be narrow enough that you can identify supervisors and papers.

## Stage 2: Choose a Project Type

Use the workbook tab `Project Selector`.

Beginner-friendly:

- case report
- audit/QI
- focused literature/scoping review
- medical education project

Intermediate:

- retrospective chart review
- systematic review
- survey study

Advanced or needs support:

- meta-analysis
- prospective clinical study
- complex database study

## Stage 3: Build a Supervisor CRM

Use the workbook tab `Supervisor CRM`.

Sources:

- placement consultants
- registrars who publish
- university academic profiles
- PubMed authors from your hospital/university
- conference abstract books
- specialty society committees
- student research societies
- hospital research office pages
- LinkedIn/X/Google Scholar profiles

For each contact, record:

- why them
- relevant work
- project angle
- email
- follow-up date
- response
- meeting date

Rule:

Do not rely on one person. Build a list of 10-20.

## Stage 4: Send Outreach

Use `Outreach Pipeline`.

Every email should answer:

- who are you?
- why them?
- what are you interested in?
- what can you help with?
- what is the easy next step?

Avoid:

- "I am interested in any research opportunities."
- long life story
- pretending you know more than you do
- asking for publication directly

## Stage 5: Supervisor Meeting

Meeting goal:

Leave with one project, one next action, and one understanding of approvals/output.

Ask:

- What is the project question?
- Is this research, audit, QI, evaluation, or case report?
- What approval/governance process applies?
- What data is needed?
- Can a student access it?
- What is my first task?
- What output are we aiming for?
- How will authorship/contribution be handled?
- When should I follow up?

If the answers are vague, the project may stall.

## Stage 6: Build Project Assets

Every project needs assets.

Case report:

- consent/local process
- clinical timeline
- literature comparison table
- CARE checklist
- case manuscript

Audit/QI:

- standard/guideline
- audit criteria table
- data collection tool
- baseline results table
- run chart/bar chart
- local presentation/poster

Retrospective study:

- protocol/brief
- inclusion/exclusion criteria
- data dictionary
- de-identified extraction sheet
- Table 1
- outcome table
- flow diagram
- STROBE checklist

Literature/scoping review:

- question framework
- search strings
- screening log
- extraction matrix
- theme/evidence table
- flow diagram if needed

Systematic review/meta-analysis:

- protocol
- PRISMA flow diagram
- study characteristics table
- risk of bias table
- forest plot if meta-analysis
- PRISMA checklist

## Stage 7: Tables and Figures First

Do not write the results section first.

Build:

- what tables will exist?
- what figures will exist?
- what data feeds them?
- what is the one message from each?

The manuscript is easier when the visual plan is clear.

## Stage 8: Manuscript Writing

Use `Manuscript Builder`.

Basic structure:

- Title
- Abstract
- Introduction
- Methods
- Results
- Discussion
- Conclusion
- References
- Tables/Figures
- Reporting checklist

Writing order:

1. Methods
2. Tables/figures
3. Results
4. Introduction
5. Discussion
6. Abstract
7. Title

## Stage 9: Submission

Use `Submission Pack`.

Before submitting:

- target journal/conference chosen
- author order confirmed
- supervisor approved final version
- ethics/governance/consent wording confirmed
- reporting guideline completed
- tables/figures formatted
- references checked
- cover letter drafted
- all files named cleanly

## Stage 10: CV Evidence

Use `CV Evidence Log`.

Research only helps your CV if it becomes evidence:

- title
- project type
- supervisor
- role
- contribution
- output
- date
- link/proof
- referee
- CV bullet
