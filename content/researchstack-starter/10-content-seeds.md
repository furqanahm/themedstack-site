# ResearchStack Content Seeds

These can become Instagram reels, carousels, TikToks, or website sections.

## Reel 1: Why Most Med Student Research Goes Nowhere

Hook:

Most medical students do not fail research because they are lazy. They fail because the project has no next action.

Points:

- vague supervisor
- vague question
- vague authorship
- vague output
- no follow-up date

CTA:

Build a research pipeline before you chase publications.

## Reel 2: The Email That Gets Ignored

Hook:

If your research email says "I am interested in research opportunities," that is probably why nobody replies.

Better structure:

- who you are
- why them
- what topic
- what you can help with
- easy next step

CTA:

Use a specific email, not a desperate one.

## Reel 3: Best First Research Projects

Hook:

If you are a med student starting research, do not begin with a huge prospective study.

Better first projects:

- case report
- audit/QI
- focused literature review
- medical education project

CTA:

Pick the project with the shortest path to a real output.

## Reel 4: Research Red Flags

Hook:

Before joining a research project, ask these questions or you may waste six months.

Questions:

- What is the output?
- What is my role?
- What approval is needed?
- Who is supervising?
- What is the next action?
- How is authorship decided?

## Reel 5: Audit vs Research

Hook:

Med students hear "just do an audit" all the time, but nobody explains what that actually means.

Points:

- audit compares practice to a standard
- QI improves a process
- research aims to generate broader knowledge
- local approval still matters

CTA:

Ask the governance question before touching data.

## Reel 6: CV Evidence

Hook:

Research does not help your CV if you cannot prove what you actually did.

Track:

- project title
- supervisor
- your role
- output
- date
- evidence
- referee/contact

CTA:

Your future CV is built from today's evidence log.

## Carousel 1: How To Start Research in Medical School

Slides:

1. Stop waiting for someone to hand you a project.
2. Pick 1-2 specialties.
3. Choose a realistic project type.
4. Build a 10-person supervisor list.
5. Read one thing they have worked on.
6. Send five specific emails.
7. Track every follow-up.
8. Ask about approvals and authorship early.
9. Aim for one output.
10. Use ResearchStack Starter.

## Carousel 2: The Supervisor Meeting Checklist

Slides:

1. What project are we doing?
2. What is the research question?
3. What approval do we need?
4. Can I access data?
5. What is my role?
6. What is the output?
7. What is the timeline?
8. How will authorship be decided?
9. What is my first task?
10. When should I follow up?
